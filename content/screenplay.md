# PARALLEL

*(working title)*

Written by Mary Stanton

FADE IN:

INT. MILA'S BEDROOM - NIGHT

A soft ambient light fills the room with gentle blue and purple hues. The space is predominantly minimalist with feminine touches - white furniture, fairy lights, a few delicate plants.

But there are strange elements scattered throughout: a small terrarium containing what appears to be bioluminescent moss, a framed diagram of quantum entanglement, a collection of strange crystals, and a bookshelf featuring titles about multiverse theory alongside fashion magazines.

MILA (23) lies on her bed, dressed in casual loungewear - comfortable but stylish. Her hair is in a messy bun. She stares at the ceiling, then reaches for her phone.

She unlocks it and taps on an app called "PARALLEL" - the icon is a simple infinity symbol with a small dot in the middle.

CLOSE ON: PHONE SCREEN

The app opens to reveal a sleek, ethereal interface. A pulsing animation ripples across the screen before settling into the home page.

The interface shows three main options:

[ PARALLELS ]  [ TIMELINE ]  [ ECHOES ]

She taps on "PARALLELS"

The screen shifts to display what looks like a dating app-style interface, but instead of strangers, it's all variations of Mila. Each profile shows a slightly different version of her - different hairstyles, clothing styles, environments.

Sliding indicators at the top of the screen show:

SIMILARITY: 78%

DIVERGENCE POINT: 4 YEARS AGO

LIFE TRAJECTORY: 56% ALIGNED

MILA (V.O.)

Sometimes I wonder who I'd be if I'd said yes instead of no.

She swipes through several versions of herself. Some look nearly identical, others dramatically different - one with a shaved head and tattoos, another in what appears to be a doctor's uniform.

She stops on one particular version - a Mila with blue-streaked hair who appears to be in a music studio. The similarity meter reads 83%.

ON SCREEN: Mila types a message.

MILA (TYPING)

Did you go to that audition?

A brief pause, then a reply appears.

PARALLEL MILA #1 (TEXT)

Yeah. Got the gig. You?

MILA (TYPING)

Never went. Got scared.

Short pause.

PARALLEL MILA #1 (TEXT)

You should have. But you're doing other cool stuff, right?

MILA (TYPING)

I guess. Nothing that feels right yet.

She swipes away from this conversation, looking momentarily contemplative.

She taps on "TIMELINE"

The screen shifts to a different interface - a vertical line with glowing dots representing different ages. Some are in the past, one is in the present (labeled "NOW"), and several extend into the future.

She taps on a dot labeled "MILA+5" (five years in the future)

The screen loads, then shows a video call interface. It connects, and we see an older version of Mila. Her hair is shorter, she has slightly more defined features, and she's in what looks like an office space with plants and art.

FUTURE MILA

Hey you.

MILA

Is it weird that I keep calling you?

FUTURE MILA

(smiling)

I used to call me too. Still do sometimes.

MILA

I just… I don't know if I'm going in the right direction.

FUTURE MILA

You never do. That's the point.

(pause)

The gallery thing works out, by the way. But not how you think.

MILA

Can you just tell me what to do next?

FUTURE MILA

You know the rules. Small nudges only.

(beat)

But I will say: that idea you just had? The one about mixing media? Yeah. That one's good. Trust it.

Mila nods, contemplative.

MILA

Thanks for…being there. Here. Whatever.

FUTURE MILA

Always. Oh and—

(glitching slightly)

Water your monstera. It's about to die.

Mila glances over at a slightly wilting plant by her window and smiles.

She ends the call and scrolls back to the main menu.

Before selecting another option, she checks her PARALLELS messages. She taps on a conversation with "PARALLEL MILA #8" (the app shows "92% SIMILARITY" next to the name). The profile shows Mila with dark red hair against a stunning mountain backdrop.

CLOSE ON: MESSAGE HISTORY

PARALLEL MILA #8 (TEXT - 3 weeks ago)

It's crazy how we're so similar but I ended up in Peru instead of staying in the city.

MILA (TEXT - 3 weeks ago)

I've always wanted to do that. What made you actually go?

PARALLEL MILA #8 (TEXT - 3 weeks ago)

Dad's health scare was different in my timeline. Made me realize life's too short. Been traveling for 14 months now.

Several more exchanges scroll by, then:

MILA (TEXT - 8 days ago)

So about that swap we talked about… what do you think? Just one day. I want to know what it feels like to be brave like you.

PARALLEL MILA #8 (TEXT - 7 days ago)

Definitely interested. I'm in a small town outside Cusco - connection might be tricky from here, but I'll try to set it up tomorrow.

MILA (TEXT - 5 days ago)

Hey, did you figure out the connection? I'm free this weekend.

MILA (TEXT - 2 days ago)

Hope everything's going alright. Still interested in swapping experiences when you're ready. No rush.

The messages end there with no response. Mila sighs softly.

She goes back to the main menu and taps on "ECHOES"

This interface is more abstract - it shows what appears to be a constellation of tiny lights, like stars or souls. Text at the top reads: "PAST LIVES & FUTURE POSSIBILITIES"

She selects one of the brighter points of light. The screen transforms into a meditation-like interface with subtle pulses. Text appears:

CONNECTING TO SOUL ECHO

LIFE PERIOD: 1892-1944

SIMILARITY: 87%

RESONANCE: EXTREMELY HIGH

Mila closes her eyes as the screen pulsates gently.

VISION SEQUENCE - INTERCUT WITH MILA'S FACE

We see fleeting images superimposed over her face - a young woman in Victorian-era clothing in a photography studio. She's behind the camera at first, photographing a handsome young man who looks at her with clear adoration.

The man says something we can't hear, gesturing for her to come around to the front of the camera. She shakes her head, shy, reluctant. He insists, gently reaching for her hand. With visible reluctance, she allows herself to be led in front of the camera.

As she poses, we notice a striking abstract painting on the wall behind her - swirls of blue and gold that seem vaguely familiar.

The man adjusts the camera, then takes her photograph as she half-hides her face, looking both shy and mysteriously alluring.

END VISION SEQUENCE

Mila's eyes open suddenly. She looks up at her wall where hangs a modern painting with similar blue and gold patterns to the one in her vision.

She grabs a journal from her desk and begins writing frantically, energized.

CLOSE ON: MILA'S JOURNAL

Her handwritten notes read:

"I was a photographer in the 1890s! Had a studio, a lover who adored me. I hated having my photo taken (still do). He insisted. The painting on the wall - almost identical to mine?? How is that possible? There was a feeling when I looked through that old camera - the same feeling I get when I take photos now…"

As she's writing, her phone CHIMES with a message notification. She glances over, surprised to see it's from PARALLEL MILA #8.

CLOSE ON: PHONE SCREEN

PARALLEL MILA #8 (TEXT)

Hey, sorry for disappearing. Something incredible happened. I was at this tiny thrift store in Cusco and felt drawn to dig through this box of old broken eyeglasses. At the very bottom, I found a photograph of my great-great-grandmother - the one I told you my family has no pictures of because she supposedly hated being photographed. I've never seen her face before. I think our worlds might be closer than we thought. I'm still down to swap.

The typing indicator appears (three dots), then disappears. Then appears again, then stops.

Mila stares at the screen, waiting.

After an uncomfortably long pause - ten, fifteen seconds - a photo attachment comes through.

Mila opens it.

CLOSE ON: THE PHOTOGRAPH

It's the EXACT photograph she just experienced being taken in her vision. The Victorian-era version of herself, half-hiding her face, in front of the abstract painting.

BACK TO MILA

She gasps, looking back and forth between the photo on her phone and her own painting on the wall.

Her hand trembles slightly as she holds the phone.

FADE TO BLACK.

Text appears:

PARALLEL

Beneath it, in smaller text:

Find Your Other Selves

FADE OUT.

THE END
